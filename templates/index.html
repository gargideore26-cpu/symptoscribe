        ϟ How it works

        How the text becomes a note

        The engine is a transparent pipeline of language-processing steps. Each one can be explained, tested and improved on its own, which matters when the output is read by a doctor.

        1. Clean and tokenise

          The text is split into sentences and words. Every word remembers where it started in the original text, so results can be highlighted exactly where the patient wrote them.

        2. Normalise spelling

          Common slips are corrected before matching: fevar becomes fever, dont becomes don't, wks becomes weeks.

        3. Find the symptoms

          Named entity recognition against a lexicon of about 40 symptoms and 300 phrasings in English and Hinglish (bukhar, sar dard, pet me dard). The longest phrase wins, plurals are handled, and unknown spellings are matched by edit distance. Patterns such as pain in my chest or left arm hurts find symptoms the lexicon does not list.

        4. Split into clauses

          Commas that separate ideas, but, and and I start a new clause, while commas inside a list (fever, cough and cold) do not. Clauses decide which details belong to which symptom.

        5. Decide the status of each symptom

          A symptom can be present, denied, uncertain, resolved, a past episode or someone else's. Negation uses a scope rule in the style of NegEx: no reaches every symptom in the list until a word like but ends it, while no relief and not improving are recognised as not being negations of a symptom. Hindi-style negation after the symptom (khansi nahi hai) is handled too.

        6. Extract the details

          Duration (for a couple of weeks, 2-3 days, since Sunday, kal se), onset, frequency (3 times a day), severity (words, 7/10, or a temperature reading), course (worsening, intermittent), triggers (after eating), body location, radiation and quality (sharp, burning).

        7. Link details to symptoms

          Details attach to the nearest symptom, a trailing detail covers the whole list (fever and cough for 3 days), and a sentence like It started two days ago is carried over to the symptom mentioned just before.

        8. Safety checks and the note

          Simple red-flag rules highlight what should not wait (chest pain, breathlessness, fever for 3 or more days, self-harm language). Missing details become follow-up questions, and a template writes the summary sentence. The tool never names a disease.

        Why rules and lexicons instead of a neural model?

        For a clinical note, a wrong answer should be explainable and fixable. Rules need no training data, behave the same every time, and show a reason for every decision (open How the engine read this text at the bottom of the analyzer). The evaluation tab measures how well they do. The natural next step is to train a model such as BioBERT or MuRIL on the same task and compare it against this baseline.

        ⚑ Limits and ethics

        Limits and ethics

        SymptoScribe organises what a patient wrote. It does not diagnose, prescribe or replace a doctor.

        What it cannot do

        • It does not suggest causes or treatments. The red flags are coarse rules for attention, not clinical triage.
        • It understands English and romanised Hindi (Hinglish). Devanagari script, Marathi and other languages are not supported yet.
        • Symptoms outside its lexicon are missed unless they match a pain in <body part> pattern. A missing symptom is not proof that nothing was said.
        • Unusual sentence structures can attach a detail to the wrong symptom. Always check the marked-up text.

        Data

        The example cases and the coverage report on the Evaluation page use the public Symptom2Disease dataset from Kaggle (1,200 patient-style symptom descriptions). Its disease labels are never used or shown to patients: SymptoScribe organises symptoms and does not predict diseases.

        Privacy

        The text is analysed in memory and not stored or sent anywhere else. Do not enter names, phone numbers or other identifying details when demonstrating the project.

        If someone is in danger

        For chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, heavy bleeding or thoughts of self-harm, contact local emergency services right away. In India, Tele-MANAS offers free mental-health support on 14416 or 1-800-891-4416.

        Future work

        • Train and compare a BioBERT or MuRIL token-classification model against this rule-based baseline.
        • Add Devanagari input and Marathi using AI4Bharat resources.
        • Add a patient-facing intake form that sends the finished note straight to the doctor.
        • Grow the annotated evaluation set with real, consented, anonymised examples.

      How it works

      How does it work?

      From the patient's own words to a note the doctor can check.

      1. 1

        Patient's text

        Typed in by the doctor, or written by the patient through a Check in link.

        On the example“No fever, but a sore throat since Sunday.”
      2. 2

        Clean the text

        Split the text into sentences and words, and fix common spelling mistakes (fevar becomes fever).

        On the exampleSplit into words. Nothing to fix here.
      3. 3

        Find symptoms

        Match words and phrases against a list of about 40 symptoms.

        On the exampleTwo symptoms: fever and sore throat.
      4. 4

        Decide status

        Decide if each one is present, denied (no fever), unsure, from the past, or about someone else.

        On the exampleFever is denied (“No”). Sore throat is present.
      5. 5

        Add details

        Attach since when, how bad, getting better or worse, where it hurts, what triggers it, and vital signs such as blood pressure.

        On the exampleSore throat: since Sunday.
      6. 6

        Check red flags

        Check for red flags with simple rules, including rules for age and pregnancy.

        On the exampleNone found.
      7. 7

        Doctor's note

        Write a short note from a template. Every fact is shown on the patient's own words, so the doctor can check it.

        On the example“Patient reports sore throat (since Sunday). Denies fever.”
      Example sentence

      “No fever, but a sore throat since Sunday.”

      Pick a step above to see what it does to this sentence.

      Plain rules, not a trained model. It supports the doctor and never diagnoses.
